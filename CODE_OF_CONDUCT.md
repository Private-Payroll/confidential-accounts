# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be decent to people. Argue with the work, not with the person doing it.

This project's review culture is direct: a refusal names what resolves it, a disagreement is recorded
with both positions, and "this is wrong and here is why" is a normal thing to read in a pull request.
**That directness is about the code. It is never a licence to be unpleasant to the person who wrote
it**, and the difference is not subtle — one names a line and what it does, the other names a person
and what they are.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in the open or in private, including unwanted contact after being asked to
  stop.
- Insults, demeaning remarks, or comments about somebody's identity or characteristics rather than
  their work.
- Publishing other people's private information.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, or reopening a settled property of the system without
  naming what has changed since it was chosen. The proposal template asks for exactly that,
  and asking a small project to re-argue a settled decision from scratch is how its attention gets
  exhausted.
- Treating a refusal as a verdict on the person who tripped it. The checks here refuse rather than
  warn, deliberately, and a review that reads *this is wrong and here is why* is doing its job. One
  that reads *you always do this* is not.
- Anything a reasonable person would read as an attempt to make somebody unwelcome here.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainers privately, through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on
this repository — the **Security** tab, **Report a vulnerability**. It is not the obvious channel for
this, and it is the only private one this project currently has; say in the title that the report is
about conduct rather than a defect.

**That is the whole of the process, and its limits are stated rather than implied.** This is a small
project. There is no separate conduct committee, no appeals body, and no promised response time. A
report is read by the people who maintain the repository, which may include the person it is about;
if that is the problem, say so and we will find another way.

## What happens

The maintainers will respond privately. Depending on what happened, that may be a conversation, a
request to change something, an edit or removal of a comment, or a block from the repository. **We
will say what we decided and why.**

## Scope

This applies in the issues, pull requests, discussions and commit messages of this repository, and to
anywhere somebody is representing this project.
